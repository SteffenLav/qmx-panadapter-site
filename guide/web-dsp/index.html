    Web DSP: Noise Reduction and Auto-Notch¶

    From v1.26.0 the web page can clean the audio before you hear it. There are three tools, and all three run in your browser:

    Tool What it does Modes
    Noise reduction - Speech model A neural network trained on speech removes what is not a voice USB, LSB, AM
    Noise reduction - Spectral Follows the noise floor in each 47 Hz slice of the audio and turns down the slices that hold only noise USB, LSB, AM, CW, CW-R
    Auto-notch Finds a steady carrier and filters it out, up to four at once USB, LSB, AM

    The Tab5 does none of this work. It sends the same audio as before, and the browser processes it. So it costs the Tab5 no CPU and no memory, it cannot disturb decoding, and it changes nothing on the Tab5's own speaker.

    Asked for by Samuel W7STF.

    Switching it on¶

    1. Open the web page and click ▶ (Listen) at the top right. The tools work on the audio that Listen plays, so there is nothing to hear without it. See Listen in the browser.
    2. Open Radio in the bottom bar. The noise reduction row is in that menu.
    3. Tick Noise reduction. Pick Low, Med or High. Picking a level also switches it on. It starts at Med.
    4. Pick Speech model or Spectral. This choice applies to the voice modes only. CW always uses Spectral.
    5. Tick Auto-notch if a carrier is in the way. It is a separate switch and works with noise reduction on or off.

    The settings are stored in the browser, so each browser and each computer keeps its own.

    A line of grey text under the row tells you when a tool is not working in the present mode:

    The row says Meaning
    loading... The browser is fetching the speech model from the Tab5
    CW uses Spectral Speech model is selected, the radio is in CW, and the page uses Spectral instead
    NR is not active in DIGI Noise reduction does nothing in this mode
    notch is not active in CW Auto-notch does nothing in this mode
    speech model could not load (...) The fetch failed. Noise reduction switches itself off. Reload the page to try again

    Which one to use¶

    You are listening to Use Why
    SSB or AM voice in band noise Speech model, Med The stronger of the two on a voice
    SSB voice, and the speech model changes the voice too much Spectral, Med It makes no assumption about the signal, so it is gentler
    CW Spectral (automatic) The speech model removes a CW note, because a note is not speech
    SSB or AM with a carrier or a tuner on the frequency Auto-notch, with or without noise reduction Noise reduction is not built to remove one tone; the notch is
    FT8, FT4, JS8, WSPR Nothing Nobody listens to them, and the decoders do not use this audio

    High is not "better Med". On a clean signal High removes part of the voice. Use it when the noise is the problem, not as a default.

    Speech model¶

    This is RNNoise (version 0.1.1, by Jean-Marc Valin / Xiph.Org, BSD licence), compiled to WebAssembly. It is a small recurrent neural network that was trained to tell speech from everything else.

    • The first time you switch it on, the browser fetches it from the Tab5: 141 KB, once. After that the browser keeps it. It works with no internet connection.
    • The level is a mix of processed and unprocessed audio. Low leaves the noise about 6 dB down, Med about 13 dB, and High passes only what the model keeps.
    • USB, LSB and AM only.

    Measured on real SSB from 40 m and 20 m, in the pauses between words:

    Level Noise in the pauses Loss in the speech itself (upper bound)
    Low about 6 dB down 2 dB
    Med about 13 dB down 4 dB
    High 19 to 31 dB down 4 to 5 dB

    The speech figure is an upper bound: the speech blocks hold noise too, and part of what is removed there is that noise. On a test voice with a 20 dB signal-to-noise ratio, High made the result worse than no processing. That is why Med is the default.

    !!! note "Why NR sounds quieter, and what is done about it" Most of the level you lose with NR on is the noise itself. On a weak signal the band noise is most of what you hear, so removing it makes the whole stream quieter. That is the intended effect.

    The voice also loses a little. Measured on the speech alone (speech-block
power minus pause power): about 1 dB with Spectral, and 1.5 / 2.7 / 3.4 dB
with the speech model at Low / Med / High. From the next release that loss
is added back after NR, so the voice keeps its loudness and only the
background goes down. CW gets no make-up: its note loses 0.3 dB.

    Spectral¶

    This is classic spectral noise reduction, written in plain JavaScript as part of the page. Nothing is fetched.

    How it works:

    1. The audio is cut into overlapping blocks and each block is split into 47 Hz slices.
    2. For each slice, the page tracks the noise floor. It takes the minimum of the smoothed power over the last 0.6 to 1.2 seconds, and it updates the floor only while the slice is near that minimum.
    3. A slice that holds more than the floor is kept. A slice that holds only the floor is turned down.
    4. The level sets the most that any slice can be turned down: Low 6 dB, Med 12 dB, High 20 dB.

    It makes no assumption about what the signal is. A CW signal is kept because it has gaps: the floor is measured in the gaps, and the note stands above it.

    A carrier that never keys has no gaps. After about one second the tracker takes it for noise and turns it down to the level's limit, in every mode.

    Measured:

    Signal Low Med High
    Steady test noise 6 dB down 12 dB down 18 dB down
    Real SSB, noise between words 4 to 6 dB down 5 to 10 dB down 6 to 14 dB down
    Real 40 m noise through the CW filter 6.0 dB down 11.7 dB down 16.3 to 16.9 dB down
    Real CW, gaps of 60 ms and longer 6 dB down 12 dB down 19 to 20 dB down
    Real CW, all key-up time including the short gaps inside a character 4.0 dB down 5.9 dB down 6.6 dB down

    Two things follow from the CW rows:

    • The gaps between words and between characters go quiet. The short gaps inside a character do not, because the tracker cannot follow a gap that short.
    • A strong note passes almost unchanged: it was 0.3 dB down.

    On a CW test signal at Med, a clear signal gains 8 to 10 dB over the noise, a weak one gains 1 to 3 dB, and a signal at the edge of copy gains nothing. A weak note is also turned down, but less than the noise is. Weak CW on the air was not measured.

    Auto-notch¶

    Auto-notch removes a steady carrier from the audio: a station tuning up, a broadcast carrier beside an SSB signal.

    • It looks between 100 Hz and 3600 Hz for a line that stands 9 dB or more above the audio near it and holds one frequency.
    • A carrier is found in about one second. The page then puts a filter 40 Hz wide on that frequency. Up to four carriers at once.
    • Measured depth on a carrier added to real speech: 31 to 59 dB.
    • About half a second after the carrier stops, the filter is removed.
    • With no carrier, the audio passes through unchanged, sample for sample. Measured on real SSB that holds no carrier.
    • A voice does not trigger it. A voice harmonic does not hold one frequency for most of a second, and the gaps between words restart the count.
    • It adds no delay.
    • USB, LSB and AM only. In CW the signal you want is a carrier.

    Short dropouts are patched¶

    The audio stream to the browser can hold short holes, about 11 ms long, when the Tab5's WiFi is busy. With noise reduction on, the page fills a hole of 8 to 58 ms with a copy of the audio just before it, faded in and out. A longer silence is left alone, because that is a closed squelch or a real outage.

    With noise reduction off, the audio is passed through untouched.

    What it costs¶
    Tab5 CPU and memory None
    Delay About 0.1 s with noise reduction on (107 ms). The notch adds none
    Browser CPU Speech model: about 1.5 % of one core, measured on a desktop PC
    Download 141 KB, once, for the speech model only
    Page size The spectral code and the notch add 6.3 KB to the page

    The delay matters for one thing: if you send CW and listen to your own signal through the browser, the sound is late. Use the radio's own sidetone for that.

    What is tested and what is not¶

    Measured Heard on the air
    Speech model on SSB Yes, on real SSB Yes
    Spectral on CW Yes, on real CW (strong signal) Yes
    Spectral on SSB Yes, on real SSB No
    Auto-notch Yes, carrier added to real SSB No

    The two rows marked No work by measurement, and nobody has yet judged them by ear on a live band. If you use them, a report of what you hear is welcome: open an issue or post in the QRP Labs group.

    Limits¶

    • One browser at a time. Only one browser gets the page's live connection, and the audio goes with it.
    • The Tab5's own speaker and headphones get no noise reduction. These tools exist only in the browser.
    • The radio's own headphone output is not affected either.
    • The speech model is trained on speech in general, not on SSB. It is not retrained for this use.
