    Gestures & Controls¶

    The entire app is controlled via one-finger swipes from screen edges and taps on the top bar.

    !!! info "This page is the authoritative list" Step 6 of the Quick Start repeats the three edge swipes as a summary, deliberately — a reader setting the device up should not have to leave that page. Change the gestures here first, and check that summary afterwards. The duplication is allowed; the two disagreeing is not.

    Edge Swipes¶

    Gesture From Effect
    Swipe → Left edge Open the page picker - tap Panadapter, FT8, FT4, JS8, WSPR or CW (CW only when enabled - see CW)
    Swipe ← Right edge Open settings drawer
    Swipe ↑ Bottom edge Open memory channel picker

    Slim breathing grip handles on these three edges show where to swipe.

    !!! note "There is no top-edge swipe any more" Earlier versions opened the spot map with a swipe down from the top edge. Removed — it collided with the top bar's own Band/Mode/BW/Zoom taps and was easy to trigger by accident. The spot map now opens from a SelfSpotter button in the settings drawer, right below Need guidance? — see the Settings Drawer section below.

    Top Bar Taps¶

    Tap any item on the top bar to open its selector:

    Audio panel¶

    Gesture From Effect
    Tap S-meter, top bar Open the audio panel

    New in v1.20.5, and extended in v1.21.0. The panel holds Mute, the Tab5 speaker volume, the radio's AF gain, the radio's RF gain and an AGC Fast / Slow / Off row, and closes on a tap anywhere outside it. It is on the S-meter because that is the one thing in the top bar already about what you are hearing. Mute is instant and is not remembered across a reboot.

    Item Selector
    Frequency Numeric keypad (MHz format)
    Mode USB / LSB / CW / DiGi buttons
    Bandwidth Filter width (SSB) or CW passband
    Bandwidth, long press CW-Tune — put the dial on the nearest CW signal within ±400 Hz (see Settings)
    Band Band list (160 m – 10 m + custom)
    S-meter Audio panel
    Zoom x1 / x2 / x4 / x8 / x16 / x24 presets; pinch for anything between

    Spectrum / Waterfall¶

    Works the same way on the Panadapter page and on the CW page (2026-11-03) - both show the live spectrum/waterfall, and these gestures were extended to CW to match. On the CW page, the left pane (macro buttons, WPM/Park/NEXT, the set toggle) is excluded: a touch that starts there is never read as a spectrum gesture.

    Gesture Effect
    Tap Tune to that frequency
    Drag (1 finger) Pan left/right. The passband marker dims to about a third while you drag rather than disappearing, so you keep something to aim at, and returns to full when you let go. It does this in every mode
    Hold ~250 ms, then drag Precise tune: a cyan cursor appears and snaps grid-to-grid as you move, and the radio follows when you lift. The hold is what separates it from a pan - a quick drag pans, a held drag tunes
    Pinch (2 fingers) Zoom in/out and pan, together - the midpoint between your two fingers drives the pan while the spread between them drives the zoom. Move both fingers with roughly steady spacing for a near-pure pan; let the spacing change and it zooms too
    Three-finger hold Toggle Simulation Mode on/off - FT8's own simulator on the Panadapter page, CW's on the CW page
    Right-drag (BLE mouse) Pan the window without moving the dial — the same gesture as the browser, and the same direction: drag right to bring lower frequencies into view. The picture slides under the pointer while the button is down and the pan is applied when you let go. Left-drag is unchanged. Works on the CW page too
    Wheel (BLE mouse) Tune in steps, using your Tune snap setting — as in the browser
    Tap a spot callsign Tune to that station and set the mode — see Live Spots
    Tap an off-screen count (<3 / 5>) Jump to the nearest spot outside the window, on this band

    Taps snap to a grid set by the mode, not the zoom level: 10 Hz in CW, 250 Hz in USB/LSB, 500 Hz in the digital modes, 1 kHz in AM.

    !!! note "Two-finger pan is live, not buttery" Unlike the one-finger drag (a local preview, settled on release), the pinch gesture recomputes the real spectrum on every tick because zoom cannot be faked locally. On this hardware a new frame can take 200-800 ms to render, so a fast two-finger drag can look like it jumps rather than slides. It is correct, just not smooth yet.

    Band-Plan Strip (Panadapter)¶

    The thin coloured strip just above the bottom status bar shows where you are within the band. The framed visible-span block on it is a slider handle you can drag to retune.

    Gesture Effect
    Tap the strip Jump to that frequency in the band
    Drag the visible-span block sideways Move the window along the band. Block, VFO marker and passband travel together and stay where you let go; the radio follows. The passband block dims while you drag instead of vanishing
    Touch just above the strip Counts as the strip — about 8 mm of the waterfall above it is a grab area, so the handle is catchable first time
    Drag from the bottom bar (sideways) Same band scrub, grabbed on/under the slider handle anywhere along the bottom status bar — a taller, easier target
    Swipe ↑ from the bottom bar Still opens the memory picker (vertical = memory, sideways = band-plan — the two share the row)

    CW Memory Buttons (CW page)¶

    Gesture Effect
    Tap Send that macro
    Press and drag up/down Re-pick which button will fire, without lifting - the highlighted one is a preview, not a commitment
    Hold 800 ms Open the editor for all six macros in the current set
    Tap the General/Contest toggle Switch macro sets (bottom of the panel)

    See CW for the macro-set editor and what each set is for.

    Web Radio¶

    Gesture Effect
    Tap a station in the list Play it
    Long-press a memory slot Store whatever is currently playing
    Tap a memory slot Play the saved station
    Tap outside the modal Close it - playback keeps running in the background

    See Web Radio for the directory search and genre filter.

    Display Sleep¶

    Gesture Effect
    Tap (screen asleep) Wake the display — the wake tap is swallowed, so it can't tune or press anything
    Two-finger double-tap Blank the display immediately

    The idle timeout is set via the Display sleep dropdown in the settings drawer (see Settings).

    Memory Channels¶

    Swipe ↑ from bottom edge -> open memory picker.

    Action Effect
    Tap channel Recall frequency, mode, and name
    Long-press channel Edit name/frequency/mode
    Long-press + drag Move the channel to a different empty slot (data follows your finger)
    Drag onto the wastebin Delete the channel — the bottom-right cell (channel 32) is a wastebin; drop a channel on it to remove it
    Tap empty slot + Save Store current frequency to that slot
    Tap occupied slot + Save Overwrite that channel

    A new device ships with a few example channels, and the first time you open the picker a one-time (~10 s) tour demonstrates the drag-to-move and drag-to-delete gestures.

    Frequency Keypad¶

    Tap the frequency label to open the keypad:

    • Digits: 0–9, . (decimal point)
    • Delete: Remove last character
    • Clear: Clear the entire entry
    • Layout toggle: Switch between 10-Key (phone dial) and Phone (QWERTY)

    Enter frequency as MHz.kHz.Hz (e.g., 14.074 for 14.074 MHz).

    FT8 Decode List¶

    Gesture Effect
    Tap row Reply to that CQ
    Long-press row (reserved for future use)
    Scroll List is scrollable; swipe up/down

    ADIF Log Viewer¶

    Gesture Effect
    Long-press row Highlight the QSO red and lock list scrolling
    Drag up/down (while highlighted) Move the highlight to another row
    Release A Delete/Cancel bar appears — Delete removes that one record
    Delete all (tap twice) First tap arms it ("ALL N?"), second tap within 5 s erases the whole log — no undo

    Filter Modal¶

    In FT8 view, tap Filter to open:

    Control Effect
    Include/Exclude checkboxes Toggle filters on/off
    Text fields Edit filter criteria
    Dropdown Change auto-reply priority
    Save Apply changes
    Cancel Discard changes

    Settings Drawer¶

    Swipe ← from right edge. The drawer is a single scrolling list of sections (they don't collapse — just scroll). It contains:

    • User Manual (top button) — opens this documentation on the Tab5 itself, at the chapter covering the screen you were on (see below)
    • Need guidance? (directly below it) — a list of symptoms and questions in plain words; picking one opens the manual at the answer. See Getting Help
    • SelfSpotter (directly below that) — opens the spot map, who is hearing your signal. All three of these top buttons go somewhere else in the app rather than tuning a setting, which is why they sit together above everything else.
    • Text fields — tap to edit (opens keyboard if needed)
    • Toggles — tap to on/off
    • Buttons — tap to open modals (Config, Time, etc.)
    • Close — swipe ← again or tap outside

    All changes are saved automatically.

    User Manual (on-device docs)¶

    The User Manual button at the top of the settings drawer opens this whole guide right on the Tab5 — no phone or laptop needed.

    It is built into the firmware (since v1.3.2), so it works immediately and always: no WiFi, no microSD card, no download, no waiting, on the very first boot. It also can never describe a different version than the one you are running, because it ships inside it.

    • It opens in context — the panadapter chapter, the FT8 receive chapter, or the FT8 transmit chapter when a transmission is armed or running. Not a contents page you have to search.
    • Contents — a two-column list of every page. Press and slide your finger down it: a highlight bar tracks your finger, and lifting opens the highlighted page.
    • Back — returns to the previous page you viewed (shown only when there is somewhere to go back to). Exit — leaves the manual (back to the panadapter/FT8 screen).
    • Edge swipes and top-bar taps are stood down while the manual is open, so a stray touch cannot retune or switch view behind it.
    • Hold the drawer's User Manual button for 3 s to reset the reader. The manual itself is untouched — it is part of the firmware and cannot be lost or go stale.
    • If a newer firmware version is available, a banner appears at the top — informational only; flashing is always your choice.

    The other two ways into the same manual — the Need guidance? panel and the tappable warning banners — are covered in Getting Help.

    Earlier firmware downloaded the manual over WiFi and could copy it to a microSD card with a green "Save offline" button. That button is gone in v1.3.2 and none of it is needed any more. An old copy left on a card is simply ignored — delete /qmx-panadapter/manual/ if you want the space back.

    Modals (Pop-up Dialogs)¶

    Standard modal controls:

    Action Effect
    Save / Apply Confirm and close
    Cancel Discard and close
    Tap outside (varies — some close, some don't)

    Each modal is self-contained — no stacking (you can't open two modals at once).

    On-Screen Keyboard¶

    Appears when you tap a text field:

    Key Effect
    Shift Three states, shown on the key itself: abc → Abc → ABC → abc. Abc capitalises the NEXT letter only and then returns to abc; ABC is a caps lock that stays until you tap it again
    Backspace Delete last character
    Space Insert space
    Numbers Tap to switch to number row
    Done / OK Close keyboard and apply

    Web UI (Browser)¶

    Left moves the radio, right moves the view. That is the whole rule for the spectrum and waterfall.

    Gesture Effect
    Left-click, or press-drag-release Tune to where you release. A cursor line follows the pointer while the button is down
    Right-drag Pan the window along the band. The dial does not move
    Scroll wheel Tune in steps, using your Tune snap setting
    Click a spot callsign Tune to that station, frequency and mode
    Drag the band-plan strip Move the window across the whole band. This one does retune
    Number fields Click to edit, press Enter

    About the right-drag. It moves the window inside what the radio is already receiving, so the frequency stays exactly where it was and the passband marker slides across the screen instead of sitting still. Drag right and you pull the spectrum right with you, bringing lower frequencies into view - the way a map drags. The passband stays visible throughout, in every mode.

    The browser's own context menu is suppressed over the spectrum and waterfall so the drag can happen; it still works everywhere else on the page.

    It needs the still display to be on, which is the default. With it off the spectrum follows the dial, so there is no separate window to move and the gesture is declined with that reason.

    The band-plan strip drags the opposite way on purpose: there your hand is on the window marker rather than on the picture, so the marker travels with your hand and the frequency rises as you drag right.

    All other web controls mirror the Tab5 display.
